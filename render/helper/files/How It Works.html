How the Render Helper works

A simple guide to what happens when you double-click Start Rendering. You don't need to understand any of it to help — this is just so nothing feels mysterious.

The 30-second version
1

Double-click Start Rendering

2

Leave it running for 1–2 hours (plugged in, lid open)

3

When it says ALL DONE, that video is finished — thank you! It then waits for the next one by itself. Press Q (or close the window) to stop.

Nothing to install. Nothing to type. Nothing is changed on your PC — and deleting one folder removes every trace.

In one sentence

Our music videos are put together from pictures, short video clips and a song. Putting them together takes a lot of computer power — so Ryan hands the job to a friend's computer (yours!), which builds the finished video and sends it back.

The big picture

  A["Ryan prepares a video job<br/>pictures, video clips and music"] --> B[("Ryan's private<br/>file server")]
  B -- "you download it" --> C["YOUR computer<br/>makes the video"]
  C -- "you send it back" --> B

Everything travels through Ryan's private server. Your computer never talks to anyone else, and nobody can reach into your computer.

What the helper does, step by step

When you open it, a window with big letters shows each of these steps as it happens, with a progress bar.

What each step does (optional)
  1. Check your computerMakes sure Windows is recent enough, you have about 15 GB free, and the built-in file-transfer tools are there. Takes a few seconds.
  2. Get the video toolsDownloads a small free program called Node.js that runs the video maker. Only the first time (a minute or two). It goes in one folder and changes nothing else on your PC.
  3. Get the EOTIR render kitThis is Ryan's own video-making recipe, so your video comes out identical to his. First time only: about 5–10 minutes.
  4. Connect and find your jobLogs in to Ryan's server with your personal key and looks for a video set aside for you. If there isn't one, it waits and checks again every few minutes, then starts by itself the moment Ryan queues one — that's fine!
  5. Download the jobPulls down the pictures, clips and music, then double-checks nothing got damaged on the way. If your internet drops, run it again — it carries on where it stopped.
  6. Make the video — the long partYour computer draws and joins up every frame. Expect 1–2 hours, a busy processor and spinning fans. That's normal and safe. The bar shows frame count and time left.
  7. Send it backChecks the finished video is the right length, uploads it to Ryan's server, makes sure every byte arrived, then deletes the big temporary files so your disk is free again. After that it looks for your next video.

What stays on your computer, and what doesn't

Stays private

  • Your files, photos and documents — the helper never looks at them.
  • Your passwords and accounts.
  • Your personal key stays on your PC (it only proves to the server that it's you).

What gets sent

  • The finished video, to Ryan.
  • If something goes wrong: a short report with an error code and basic info (Windows version, free space, what step failed). No personal files.

If something goes wrong

Don't worry — nothing on your computer is harmed. The window will show a big ERROR CODE (like E-RENDER) and a plain explanation. The helper also sends a report to Ryan automatically, so you can just tell him the code. You can always double-click again; it picks up where it left off.

Keeping it tidy

  • While it works: keep the window open, keep your computer plugged in and don't close the lid. It keeps going from one video to the next; to stop, press Q (it finishes the video it's on first) or just close the window. You can use your PC normally — it may feel a bit slower.
  • Remove everything: delete the folder %LOCALAPPDATA%\Eotir (paste it into File Explorer's address bar). That's every trace of it. (A few PCs use C:\Users\Public\Eotir instead — the window tells you.)
  • Curious what it does exactly? The file eotir-render.ps1 is plain readable text — open it in Notepad.
